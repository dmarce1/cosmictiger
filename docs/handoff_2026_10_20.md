## 3. The CUDA kick program in one page

Everything since 2026-10-15 is one program: the order-aware shared-memory
CUDA kick arena, documented in `docs/cuda_kick_shared_memory.md` (2,759 lines;
each measured step is a `###` section with its result folder named in the
heading, and `### What remains` at the end is the live to-do list). Timings

in paired runs; `benchmarks/check_kick_arena_pairs.py` gates a stage on its
worst pair spread, `compare_kick_arena_binaries.py` checks force exports
bit for bit, `compare_kick_arena_runs.py` makes the paired speedup tables.

Benchmark build configuration (every frozen binary): Release, Ninja,
`-DCMAKE_CUDA_COMPILER=/usr/local/cuda/bin/nvcc -DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=86`,
`COSMICTIGER_WITH_MAX_BUCKET=1024`, `COSMICTIGER_WITH_ORDER=7` where the
tree has it, STF benchmarks on, tests on, HPX from `~/local/hpx/Release`,
GSL/CHEALPIX from `/tmp/cosmictiger_sph_deps/root/usr`. A full build at `-j6`
takes about five minutes; a `gravity_cuda.hpp` change about 90 s.

Current defaults on main, decided from measurement:

- Handoff divisor: `kickBlockCount(N) = 2 * SMs * clamp(cbrt(N / 32768), 1, 3)`
  (164 blocks at 32,768 particles, 328 at 262,144, 492 at and above 884,736).
- `compute.cudaKickFineHandoffBlocks` exists but defaults to 0 (off): the
  Ewald-aware finer cut gains on the clustered fixture and loses on the
  adaptive one until workitems are dispatched largest first (section 4.4).
- Residency 8 blocks per SM, list reservation 4096 plus the per-launch list
  slab, every inner loop reads staged planes and records through the
  `extern __shared__` symbol, the Fourier sum of the Ewald cell-cell
  derivatives is evaluated before the real-space images.
- Order 7 at theta 0.8 is the benchmark configuration, not a production
  recommendation: the theta sweep showed lower orders at smaller angles reach
  the same force error cheaper down to about 2e-4.

### 4.2 Lattice CPU/GPU Ewald agreement and the radial-term series (worktree quizzical-wilson-9408a4)

Session "Check CPU and GPU Ewald agreement on the lattice"
(`local_47c71c7e-f5f8-4c21-94c7-a57c9e931f96`). Status: investigation
concluded, fix validated, timing done, doc section drafted, NOT committed.
Note: `.../side-session-notes/local_4eecff9b-d262-462e-9e01-1b23f90ce3d1/quizzical_wilson_lattice_ewald_agreement.md`
(last updated 04:50, before the conclusion; the drafted doc section is the
authoritative write-up, see the patch).

Question answered: why the lattice fixture's forces differ between handoff
divisions (492 vs 656 blocks at 2.1 M particles: potential rel L2 4.4e-4),
when the clustered and adaptive fixtures agree at 6e-8. Answer (drafted
section "Lattice agreement between handoff divisions (measured)", inserted
after "CPU and CUDA agreement"): float rounding. Reversing the order in which
the CPU walk sums its Ewald list moves the lattice by the same amount; the
lattice potential is a small residual of sums of order N, so one float
rounding of 2.4 N against 2.84 times the side is 5e-5 to 8e-4 at sides 32 to
128 and the measured differences are about half that. Against the direct
Ewald sum both divisions have the same error to four digits, so neither side
is more accurate. The lost-source-trace hypothesis was tested and refuted.

Defect found on the way, shared by CPU and GPU: `ewaldCorrectionDerivatives`
built the nearest image's erf(alpha r)/r radial terms by an upward recurrence
that cancels catastrophically for alpha r below one (float d_4, d_5, d_6
wrong by 0.97, 85 and 8,900 times their size at the box/8 separation). The
generator now sums a 20-term power series for S_6 and recurs downward; worst
relative error of any d_l over 0.01 to 0.866 drops from 6e28 to 1.4e-6 at
order 7. `tests/stf_ewald_cpu.cpp` gains `checkFloatDerivatives`, every rank
of the float derivatives against double at eight separations (tolerance
5e-5 of the rank's scale). `benchmarks/stf_walk.cpp` now writes
`stf_walk_reference.csv` so CPU-only runs can be compared to a CUDA run's
direct reference. The fix does not move the lattice difference (2.37e-5 and
8.81e-5 instead of 2.37e-5 and 8.99e-5) nor any fixture's error against the
direct sum by more than 2e-4 of itself; the kernel keeps 255 registers and
has 56 fewer instructions.

Timing head 1bb812d0 vs series, bucket 64, order 7, five pairs
(`lattice_ewald_agreement/timing/`, worst pair spread 1.117): level on all
three fixtures (lattice 14.6 vs 14.7 ms, clustered 5.8 vs 5.8, adaptive 11.0
vs 11.0). Forces differ from head by rel L2 8e-6 (lattice) and under 4e-7
elsewhere, as expected from corrected high ranks.

## 7. Rules and pitfalls to honour when resuming

- Everything develops on `main`; the user is the only contributor, no pull
  requests; a branch is your scratch space and gets merged back. When another
  checkout has `main` checked out, advance it with
  `git -C <checkout> merge --ff-only <sha>`, never `git update-ref` (that
  leaves the checkout's index at the old commit and the next commit there
  reverts your change; it happened twice on 2026-10-20).
- Fresh configures need `-DCMAKE_CUDA_COMPILER=/usr/local/cuda/bin/nvcc
  -DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=86` even for CPU-only builds; delete a failed
  build directory before retrying.
- At most 12 build or test jobs combined across all sessions; check `ps` for
  other `ninja` processes first. One ninja per build directory.
- GPU timing: one job on the workstation at a time. Wait for quiet first
  (no ninja, compiler, ncu, nsys, sanitizer; no GPU compute client; fewer
  than four cores busy by `/proc/stat`, not by load average, because the
  user's rsync keeps the load near 4 to 12 in IO wait), then take
  `/home/dmarce1/.config/Claude/side-session-notes/local_1aab8699-560a-48fb-895b-1e8a0aa36de6/gpu_benchmark.lock`
  with `flock -w 14400`, never hold it while waiting. Prefer five pairs; the
  first process of a kernel-bound stage runs 10 to 15% slow while the GPU
  clock ramps, so warm up before pair 0. Read `wall_seconds` and tree times
  in `runs.json` to spot disturbed runs; `check_kick_arena_pairs.py
  --max-spread 1.15` (1.2 at 2.1 M particles).
- At side 128 every process writes a 945 MB `stf_walk_forces.csv`; delete
  them only after the driver prints "Completed" (its summary re-reads them),
  and watch `/home` (66 GB free).
- `pkill -f`/`pgrep -f` with a literal pattern matches the Bash tool's own
  shell and kills it. Keep patterns in a file and kill by PID. A running
  cosmictiger shows to `pgrep -x` as `main-thread#0`; gate on
  `nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps`.
- Killing another session's background task or `tail -f` watcher wakes that
  session, which may relaunch its driver. Stop the session's turn and message
  it first.
- The gaming cap: when the user says in their own words that they are
  playing War Thunder, all sessions together use at most 6 threads and a
  quarter of the GPU and run no paired timings, ncu or nsys; it lifts only on
  the user's own words. It was OFF at shutdown.
- QB4 cluster access is paused; no SSH to qbd.loni.org under any pretext.
- compute-sanitizer racecheck reports "invalid memcpy_async synchronization"
  for cp.async plus cuda::barrier staging whenever ptxas emits
  `ARRIVES.LDGSTSBAR.64 [URn+imm]`; it is a tool artefact, documented in the
  design doc. Bound its output with `--print-limit`.
- This account hit its monthly API spend limit at about 05:20 CDT on
